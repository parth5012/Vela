Three design worlds for Vela

Three materially different visual systems for the same single-owner, self-hosted AI assistant. All render the identical chat screen (analyst persona, real default copy) so the comparison stays about direction, not layout. All three honor the constraints: dark-only, WCAG AA contrast on text, Material 3 structure/navigation, no drop-shadow reliance, 48dp-class touch targets. None is a proposal you must accept — each names its honest risk.

Synthetic demo content: real app strings (greeting, starter prompts, personas, model pills, typing label) come from client/store/useConfigStore.ts and client/app/index.tsx.

Variant A · Operate

Signal

Material 3 done right — tonal surfaces, one indigo accent, calm native trust.

The assistant fades into the OS. Composition, elevation and touch come straight from Material 3; the agent's disclosure (thought, tools, sources) is the only ornament.

Variant B · Operate

Phosphor

A terminal-grade command deck for the person who runs their own infra.

Your server, your agent, your shell. Mono micro-labels, hairline grids and a phosphor amber cursor turn every disclosure — thought, tool call, source — into a legible terminal readout.

Materials & language

  • Mono micro-labels (uppercase, tracked) over a sans body
  • Hairline borders + 4px radii, square send button, block cursor typing
  • Phosphor amber at 11.8:1, body text at 14.6:1 — AA comfortably

Signature interaction

  • The typing indicator is a blinking block cursor with a mono "COMPILING" label — the stream, read as a process.
Risk: the terminal poetry can wear thin in everyday chat; needs the sans body and generous spacing to stay an operating tool, not a costume.
Variant C · Experience

Aurora

A night-sky glass layer for the assistant you trust after dark.

The client is the window, so make it a window: frosted panels over a deep indigo sky, an aurora accent running violet→cyan, a serif voice for the greeting. Disclosure glows instead of shouting.
